PACKAGE 1 — FLORES DOUBLE A-GAP / FREE RUNNER

Minnesota vs Green Bay. Blitz on 40 of 46 dropbacks (PFF) or 78% (NGS). 53% pressure. 32 pressures. 15 hits. 4 sacks. Dallas Turner 10 pressures and 7 hits. Blake Cashman came untouched on the late INT. Four of five Packers linemen allowed pressure inside 2.50 seconds.

The look: Five and six at the line. Love called it exotic and still could not ID the extra man. Green Bay's answer was to leave a free rusher from the blindside — the man least likely to hit the ball as it came out. That worked early (Watson 147). It died in the fourth (Love 31.6 rating vs blitz).

Attack: Hot throw to the vacated hook, or a back in protection. GB did the first for three quarters and then stopped winning the math. Next QB vs Flores: if the line cannot live at 2.5 seconds, take Minnesota and the WR who wins the vacated zone.

PACKAGE 2 — QUINN 50% PRESSURE, NOT FLORES VOLUME

Dan Quinn was the only other coordinator over 50% (50.9%). Washington lost 24-22 and still covered +5.5. This is not Minnesota. It is enough pressure to keep Hurts from pulling away, not enough to end the game.

Attack: Next time Quinn is over 45%, the dog with points is live if the offense can stay inside a field goal. Do not treat 50% like 82%.

PACKAGE 3 — GLENN AND MINTER MULTIPLE, NOT ALL-OUT

Jets 44.9%. 2-4-5 nickel, edges in two-point stance. Three sacks, Ward 140 yards, 195 yards allowed. The blitz is a changeup. The four-man and Hall's clock are the identity.

Ravens 43.4%. Hendrickson 58.6% pressure when he is on the field, 20% when he is not. That is a four-man package wearing a blitz rate. Jones 149 pass yards. Taylor still 98-2.

Attack: Vs Glenn — do not assume a shootout. Vs Minter — the extra rusher is often Hendrickson, not a sixth body. Run it if you have a back. Do not live downfield.

PACKAGE 4 — FANGIO SIM PRESSURE (SHOW FIVE, SEND FOUR)

Eagles and Cowboys led the league in simulated pressure in the preseason (19% and 18%). Week 1 Philly had one sack (Campbell) and Hunt had seven pressures. Dallas nickel leaked 215 yards. Sim pressure is the show. The rush still has to get there.

Attack: If the fifth man drops, throw the hook he left. Giants and Dart did that to Dallas nickel. Commanders stayed inside a field goal against Philly. Next Fangio/Parker dive: the vacated zone is the ticket, not fading the whole defense.

PACKAGE 5 — FOUR-MAN THAT PLAYS LIKE A BLITZ

Bengals — Lawrence inside, rush four, four takeaways, Mayfield strip, defensive TD. Lowest-blitz identity last year. Week 1 they still wrecked Tampa without needing a sixth rusher.

Jaguars — Hines-Allen + Walker, five sacks, Browns 1-of-8 third down. Same idea. The package is the front, not the extra LB.

Raiders — Five sacks. Jeanty scored. Pressure plus a featured back.

Steelers — Watt is the package. Pick-six, two sacks. Ugly 20-13.

Attack: Do not force a shootout total against these four. Isolate the back or TE. The QB stack is the field's mistake.

PACKAGE 6 — NO BLITZ, AND IT SHOWED

Rams 0 sacks. Dolphins 0 sacks (Jeanty two receiving TDs). Chargers 1 sack, lost outright as a 9-point favorite. Houston static Wide-9, almost no disguise — Allen 7.9 yards per play.

Attack: Dual-threat or featured back vs a no-blitz front. That is the side and the DFS engine.

HOW TO WRITE THIS INTO A BREAKDOWN
  • Name the package: double A-gap, sim five-send-four, four-man wrecking crew, or no-blitz static.
  • One number: blitz rate, time to pressure, or sacks from the front four.
  • Say who it cooked and who it missed.
  • If the next opponent's line lives inside 2.5 seconds, do not pick them to cover vs Flores.
